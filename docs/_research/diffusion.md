<strong>In brief.</strong> Training a model to find diseases on CT normally needs expert voxel labels. We asked whether a text-to-image diffusion model, trained only on scans paired with their radiology reports, could point to a disease named in text, with no segmentation labels. The model learned to generate realistic chest CT from a report, but its attention maps did not find the disease.
</div>

## Approach

- **Data.** 25,692 chest CT scans with reports (CT-RATE), resampled to 1 mm voxels, registered to a reference scan and cropped to 256³.
- **Compression.** A two-stage 3D VAE, trained first on patches and then on whole volumes, compresses each scan to a 64³ latent.
- **Generator.** MedFlux, a diffusion transformer (12 blocks, width 768) in which image tokens and report tokens attend to each other in every block.
- **Localization.** ConceptAttention adds a token for a disease name such as "pleural effusion"; its similarity to each image patch gives a heat map, with no extra training.

## Results

| Model | FID ↓ | MS-SSIM |
|---|---|---|
| MedSyn | 0.0203 | 0.2842 |
| MAISI | 0.0179 | 0.2631 |
| 3D MedDiffusion | 0.0102 | 0.2468 |
| **MedFlux (ours)** | 0.0176 | 0.2575 |

After 40,000 training steps, MedFlux ranked second in FID, behind 3D MedDiffusion. It followed prompts for a normal scan and for bilateral pleural effusion, but it did not draw small nodules.

## What we learned

ConceptAttention was shown on image models trained on an estimated billion-plus images. With about 20,000 scans, each far larger than a photo, our model had not learned enough meaning for its attention to point at. Our proposed next step was to have an LLM reduce each report to a short list of findings, so the model can learn which words go with which anatomy.
